## Findings and dispositions

### Critical

- **The PostgreSQL spike does not compile, and `cargo check --workspace` is blind to it.** `impl UploadMeta for PgStore` in `spike/pg-meta/src/lib.rs` was missing the required trait item `UploadMeta::list_parts_page` (a required method with no default body), which the contract gained in Task 9's D10 amendment and which was never back-filled into the spike. The crate lives **outside the workspace** (`spike` is in the root `Cargo.toml`'s `exclude`) and in **no CI job**, so no gate in this repository builds it and Task 2's "all green" verdict is unreproducible from the committed source. (2026-10-07: the operator ruled a compile-only CI job in — `pg-meta-check`, running `cargo check --manifest-path spike/pg-meta/Cargo.toml` — superseding "in no CI job"; the spike stays outside the workspace and outside every gate that runs the suite.) The same mechanism then fired a **second time mid-fix-wave**: the fix wave added three more required methods with no default bodies — `BucketMeta::set_tags_or_create`, `BucketMeta::set_grants_or_create` and `UploadMeta::drain_upload_checked`, the atomic first-sight element writes and the abort path's identity-checked drain that the fs-side transaction degradation below required — and the spike was broken again before being back-filled. All are implemented now, but implementing them restores the crate's **source**, never its **verdict**. Review Focus item 1 exists to prevent exactly this inversion in the other direction (a contract the gate never exercised being relied on); the spike's own compile break is the mirror case, and `docs/superpowers/pg-meta-spike-findings.md` now opens with the warning that says so.

### The contract grew during P1 without its gate being re-run

- The gate covers the contract surface as it stood when Task 2 ran. Task 9's D10 amendment then added `list_parts_page`, and the fix wave added the three atomic-write methods above; `set_tags_or_create`, `set_grants_or_create` and `drain_upload_checked` have never been exercised by a spike run, and `list_parts_page` was never exercised either. The recorded 8/8 verdict therefore covers **less** of the landed contract surface than it claims. The remedy is procedural and is stated in the spike record: bring the database up, run the gate, and re-paste its output — the spike's findings document must not be read as a green verdict for the landed contract until then.

### Live regressions in the wired paths

- **The `became_noncurrent` stamp was read in a separate transaction from the write that consumes it.** At HEAD the stamp was read inside the put transaction; the migration moved the read out, so a concurrent supersede on the same key can race it. fs masked the window behind the bucket mutation lock; mem would not have. **Fixed**: `put_version` now preserves the stored stamp inside its own write transaction (`crates/tinio-store-redb/src/meta/version.rs`), and the engine's now-pointless read was deleted — which also removed two extra point reads per supersede.
- **`list_uploads_page` had lost the UUID identity guard its siblings kept.** `crates/tinio-fs/src/multipart.rs`'s paged listing served rows without the `Uuid::parse_str` filter that `list_uploads` and `walk_uploads` still applied, so a tampered or corrupt `UPLOADS` row could be served by `ListMultipartUploads`. **Fixed**: the guard is restored, pinned by `the_paged_listing_drops_a_non_uuid_upload_row`.

### Transaction degradation

- **Three fs operations went from one write transaction to two or three**, against the plan's Global Constraint that every multi-table operation is one write transaction: the multipart `abort` path (its identity check and its drain), bucket `set_tags`, and bucket `set_acl`. The operator ruled that the contract should gain the methods that express them atomically rather than accept the split — hence `UploadMeta::drain_upload_checked`, `BucketMeta::set_tags_or_create` and `BucketMeta::set_grants_or_create` above, each wired into the fs call site (`crates/tinio-fs/src/bucket.rs`, `crates/tinio-fs/src/multipart.rs`).

### A reachable panic, a latent deadlock, and a staging-file leak

- **A reachable panic on a public value**: `From<PartRow> for multipart::ObjectPart` called `.expect("a stored part number is in range")` on a stored part number, with the pair on the wrong directions — the infallible `From` was the one that could panic and the fallible `TryFrom` could never fail. **Fixed**: the pair is `TryFrom<PartRow>` (refusing with `storage::Error::InvalidPartNumber`) and `From<&multipart::ObjectPart>`, so the panic is gone and the refusal is where a caller can see it.
- **A latent deadlock in `ObjectContent::rename`**: it took the bucket-mutation lock **unconditionally** while `publish` branched on `own_lock`. The repo documents the hazard itself (`crates/tinio-fs/src/backend/version.rs`: taking the lock again on the task that already holds it "is a deadlock, not a critical section"), and the per-key lock is not re-entrant. **Fixed**: `rename` now branches on `own_lock` exactly as `publish` does; the mutation test above proves the deadlock was real by timing out at 5.02 s.
- **A staging-file leak in `publish`**: the `ContentKey::Part` arm took the temp out of the staging value and then had three error paths that orphaned it, with the upload-id validation happening only after the temp existed. **Fixed**: the temp is cleaned on every failure path and the upload id is validated before the temp is created; pinned by `a_failed_part_publish_leaves_no_temp`.

### A contract the gate proves but nothing depends on

Recorded as remaining rather than fixed here.

## Corrections

- **The round refuted one of its own claims.** The A2 "stamp" finding was first relayed with the wrong mechanism: the claim was that a row corrupt *only* in its blob/id/kind wire kept its stamp at HEAD and is silently un-stamped now. That is false, and was refuted by reading both decoders — HEAD's raw read decoded the same three elements and rejected the same set, so no such row class exists. The real defect is only the lost atomicity (the stamp read left the write transaction), and the fix that landed — `put_version` preserving the stored stamp inside its own write transaction, with the engine's now-pointless read deleted — is the correct fix for the real defect.
- **A rust-analyzer diagnostic ("unused import: `self`") was acted on and was wrong.** The compiler refuted it immediately and the edit was reverted. Lesson recorded: in this workspace, act on `rustc`/`clippy` output, not editor diagnostics.
- **`VersionMeta::drain_bucket` landed after this review, and its first signature was wrong and was corrected.** The first pass was `Result<(), <Self as Metadata>::Error>` with a `where Self: Metadata` clause — which made it an outlier among `VersionMeta`'s twelve others (all plain `Self::Error`) and forced `assert_version_conformance`'s bound to widen to `V: VersionMeta + Metadata`. It now uses `Self::Error`, the widening was reverted (the suite's bound is again `V: VersionMeta`), and the two `drain_bucket` calls in that suite are qualified as `VersionMeta::drain_bucket(store, &bucket)` because the test store implements `ObjectMeta`/`UploadMeta`/`VersionMeta` and all three now carry a `drain_bucket`. The method itself is described under Remaining, below.

## The PostgreSQL gate ran, failed, and was fixed (2026-10-07, after this review)

- **The gate compiled, connected and passed its first two legs.** `cargo run --manifest-path spike/pg-meta/Cargo.toml` against a real `postgres:18-alpine` (server 18.4, `server_encoding=UTF8`, `datcollate=en_US.utf8`) **compiled and connected** — the earlier compile fixes held — and legs 1–2 passed, including the collation pin demonstrated on the same values: `COLLATE "C"` orders `["0","B","_","a"]` against `COLLATE "en-US-x-icu"`'s `["_","0","a","B"]`.
- **Leg 3 panicked** inside `assert_metadata_conformance` on the `max = 0` invariant of `list_uploads_page` (the operator's panic named `crates/tinio-util/src/testing/metadata.rs:1913`; that assertion has since moved down the file as legs were added, to around :1975). The failing condition was `truncated == true`: `PgStore::list_uploads_page` entered its loop with `max_uploads == 0`, evaluated `0 + 0 >= 0`, set `truncated = true` and broke — returning a truncated page that named **no** continuation marker, which is not resumable.
- **Root cause and fix.** The spike had hand-rolled the entire pager, re-implementing rules that already live in one place — `tinio_core::storage::group_and_paginate_unordered` (`crates/tinio-core/src/storage/listing.rs`), whose doc records that it owns the marker, rollup, truncation and resume rules and that `max = 0` short-circuits there. Redb already delegates to it; the spike did not. The operator ruled the spike should **adopt the shared helper** rather than patch the `max = 0` case, so it inherits every rule instead of restating them. That is landed, and the spike's private `rollup` helper was deleted with it. The contract's `list_uploads_page` doc also gained the rule (it had it for `list_parts_page` and not for its sibling), including that a truncated page always carries its resume marker. Consequence: the spike's page marker is now the composite `key\0upload_id` form (`storage::uploads_order`) instead of the bare upload id it used before — matching redb/mem/fs and the fs adapter's split round-trip.
- **The `list_parts_page` observation.** It still hand-writes a `max == 0` early return that duplicates the arm `storage::paginate_ordered` owns — but the redb reference hand-writes the same arm, so the spike matches its sibling; left as-is, per the operator's scope.
- **The performance item the operator deferred.** Both redb and the spike materialize the whole bucket's uploads and paginate in memory (redb by reading every row, the spike formerly by an unlimited `SELECT`). The operator ruled this is **not** fixed now — bounding it needs a cursor-style change in the shared pager and bounding only one implementation would diverge at the delimiter-rollup boundary — and recorded it as finding **F17** in `docs/superpowers/pg-meta-spike-findings.md` for the `crates/tinio-store-pg` promotion.
- **What remains unverified.** Only legs 1–3 of 8 ever executed; legs 4–8 have **never** run against any database, and leg 3 has not been re-run since the fix. The operator must re-run (`docker compose -f spike/pg-meta/docker-compose.yml up -d --pull never`, then `cargo run --manifest-path spike/pg-meta/Cargo.toml`). The spike's compile can be checked without a database — `cargo check --manifest-path spike/pg-meta/Cargo.toml`, which currently exits 0 — and that is the check the workspace gate cannot perform (`cargo check --workspace` cannot see a crate outside the workspace), which is why the same defect class got through twice. **Corrected 2026-10-07:** "Only legs 1–3 of 8 ever executed" and "legs 4–8 have **never** run against any database" are false - the 2026-10-03 run completed all eight legs. ## The PostgreSQL gate ran a second time, and the rule-versus-implementation audit (2026-10-07, after the section above)

The section above is the first run and stands as written; the gate then ran again, and this is what it did.

- **Legs 1–3 passed.** The operator re-ran `cargo run --manifest-path spike/pg-meta/Cargo.toml` against the same real PostgreSQL 18.4. Leg 3, `assert_metadata_conformance`, went **green** — the uploads-page fix worked, and the deepened `list_uploads_page` legs now pass on a non-redb backend.
- **Leg 4, `assert_version_conformance`, then panicked**: `a row naming different content at the same sequence is refused` (the operator's panic named `crates/tinio-util/src/testing/metadata.rs:465`, where that assertion still sits).
- **Root cause.** `PgStore::put_version` was an unconditional `INSERT … ON CONFLICT (bucket, key, seq) DO UPDATE` with no guard at all, so it silently overwrote another version's committed row — and it violated **three** documented rules at once, not one: the same-sequence refusal (a row at a seq may be rewritten only by the same identifier, kind **and** content — blob plus byte count — because overwriting drops that version's blob out of the live set, silently and unrecoverably); the rule that the **stored** `became_noncurrent` stamp is the authority on a rewrite and the caller's value is ignored (the SQL took `EXCLUDED.became_noncurrent`, the very defect fixed in the redb implementation days earlier); and the null-identity displacement rule (committing the null identity must remove any other null-id row of the key together with its index entry, in the same transaction — the suite asserts that leg immediately afterwards, so fixing only the first would have failed next).
- **Fix.** One transaction with a `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` read of the row at `row.seq` at its head (the contract needs three decisions from the stored row, and the stamp and the displacement target must not race a concurrent commit), a new typed refusal `Error::VersionConflict { bucket, key, seq }` following this crate's typed-refusal shape and projecting to `storage::Error::Io` as its `PublishRaced` sibling does, the stored stamp bound into the upsert instead of `EXCLUDED`, and a data-modifying CTE for the displacement. The spike's `put_version` **already** wrote the `version_index` row in the same transaction, so that was not a second omission. Recorded as finding **F18** in `docs/superpowers/pg-meta-spike-findings.md`, with the per-method walkthrough row corrected.
- **The audit that followed.** Because the gate had now failed three times on rules only the redb implementation carried, the same wave ran a rule-versus-implementation audit of all five contracts — every doc bullet that states a *rule*, checked against the spike's implementation. Two conclusions: **required-method completeness is closed** (every method of `BucketMeta`, `ObjectMeta`, `UploadMeta`, `VersionMeta` and `ObjectContent` is present in all four implementors, so the compile-level gap class that broke the crate twice is gone and what remains is rule-level only), and **exactly three rule-level divergences remained**, none of them exercised by the shared suite (so the gate was green or red independently of them): `ensure_tags`' "replacing an identical set **without a write**" (the spike issued an unconditional `UPDATE`; redb uses `write_if(changed)`, committing nothing); `advance_slot`'s "a slot never moves backwards" refusal (redb gained that guard with a test this week; the suite only ever advances forward — 1, 2, 3, 9 — which is why no leg caught it); and `expire`/`expire_many`'s "remove the row at `seq` and **its** index entry" (the spike deleted the index row by identity alone; redb's `remove_pointing_at` deletes only when the entry actually points at that sequence). The last two are defensive rather than live: the identity-only delete is safe only because the enforced one-null-version rule leaves one row per identity wire, and `advance_slot` has no production caller at all.
- **All three are fixed** in the spike (a conditional `UPDATE`; a `FOR UPDATE` slot read plus a new typed `Error::SlotRewind` mirroring redb's projection; `AND seq = $n` on the index deletes), recorded as finding **F19**. ## Remaining, and what was decided about each

- **Codec drift** converged on `tinio-core`; `decode_tags_wire` gained its named core home; the two new setters' "unchanged write" semantics and the first-sight empty-grants consequence are documented in the trait docs (`crates/tinio-core/src/meta/bucket.rs`).
- **`VersionMeta::drain_bucket` landed; the fs teardown is deliberately not re-pointed onto it, and that is why it has no production caller yet.** The contract gained `async fn drain_bucket(&self, bucket: &bucket::Name) -> Result<(), Self::Error>;` on `VersionMeta` (`crates/tinio-core/src/meta/version.rs`), documented as the bucket being the unit, the row and its index entry retiring together, the call being idempotent, and a bucket with no version row answering `Ok`. `crates/tinio-store-redb/src/meta/version.rs` runs it in one `driver.write` closure calling `VersionTable::drain_bucket` then `IndexTable::drain_bucket` — one write transaction, the existing table methods. All four implementors carry it: `RedbMetadata` (above); `Rows` in `crates/tinio-fs/src/version_adapter.rs` (pure forwarding); `Fake` in `crates/tinio-version/src/testutil.rs` (`unimplemented!` — the engine never drains, per that double's documented convention); and `PgStore` in `spike/pg-meta/src/lib.rs` (a real `client.transaction()` with two bound-parameter `DELETE`s). The suite leg sits at the end of `assert_version_conformance` (`crates/tinio-util/src/testing/metadata.rs`): it plants rows for a third key plus a second bucket, drains, asserts every read answers empty, the other bucket survives, and a second drain is a no-op. **Why nothing calls it yet:** `FsStorage::remove_bucket_state` (`crates/tinio-fs/src/backend/mod.rs`) is **one** `write` transaction that removes the bucket row and drains `OBJECT_META`, `OBJECT_PART`, `VERSION_ROWS`, `VERSION_INDEX`, `BUCKET_VERSIONING` and the upload tables together, with the block subtree removed after it.
